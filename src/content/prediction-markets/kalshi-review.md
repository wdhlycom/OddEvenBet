Kalshi currently has the clearest compliance identity of any prediction market in the US: it is regulated by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) and was one of the first exchanges to receive this kind of approval. For the risk-averse, that matters more than anything else.

## What Kalshi actually is

A CFTC-regulated event-contract exchange that settles in US dollars (fiat). Unlike Polymarket's offshore crypto structure, Kalshi sits inside an explicit US regulatory framework, so you can fund in dollars and never touch wallets, keys or stablecoins.

This positioning dictates all of its strengths and weaknesses: **compliance and safety are the selling point; depth and market breadth are the shortfall.**

## Fees

Kalshi's approach to fees isn't identical to Polymarket's, and the rules shift with the market. The overall principle: **it leans toward the charging logic of a "regulated exchange" rather than a "maker-friendly order book."**

For the occasional better, fees are not decisive; for high-frequency small orders, they accumulate fast. Confirm the rate on the specific market page before you order.

## Market coverage

This is Kalshi's clearest shortfall:

- **Strong**: US politics (elections, Congress, Fed decisions), macro (inflation, rates), weather, entertainment. These US themes are rich.
- **Weak**: sports, crypto, global events, niche events — nowhere near as complete as Polymarket.

If you mainly bet US macro events like "will the Fed hike next month", Kalshi works well; if you want to bet some obscure crypto event, it basically doesn't have one.

## Compliance and safety

This is Kalshi's biggest moat. CFTC regulation means:

- A named regulator stands behind the operation — somewhere to appeal when something goes wrong.
- Fiat in and out, with funds moving through traditional financial channels.
- Its compliance identity lets it serve US users openly, without fear of sudden bans.

For many beginners this alone beats Polymarket — **you don't have to study what a self-custody wallet is, what USDC is, or what happens if you lose your keys.**

## Who should and shouldn't use it

**Choose Kalshi:**

- You're in the US and want to fund directly in dollars.
- You value compliance and don't want to touch crypto or wallets.
- You mainly bet US political and macro events.

**Don't choose Kalshi:**

- Overseas users (essentially unusable).
- You want to bet sports, crypto or niche events.
- You're chasing maximum depth and liquidity.

## An honest caveat

Kalshi's "compliance" also brings limits: it serves US users only, and overseas identities are almost impossible to open an account with. If you're in mainland China or another non-US region, this path doesn't work — which sends you back to offshore platforms like Polymarket, or to skipping the category entirely.
