The biggest trap of your first prediction-market experience isn't "calling the direction wrong" — it's "betting big before you've walked the flow through." This page takes you through the full path from opening an account to your first trade at minimal cost.

## Step 1: Pick the right platform

In 2026, beginners face two main choices:

- **Kalshi**: US-regulated (CFTC), funded via bank transfer or debit card, friendliest to beginners. For US users.
- **Polymarket**: crypto-native, trades in USDC (a dollar-pegged stablecoin), available globally but restricted for US users. For overseas users who know a little crypto.

**A simple rule**: if you're in the US, start with Kalshi; if you're not, or already know crypto wallets, start with Polymarket. Don't open both at once — get comfortable with one first.

## Step 2: Fund with what you can afford to lose

$50–200 is plenty. At this stage your goal is "learn", not "earn". Remember: **treat this money as tuition — losing it all shouldn't affect your life.**

- Kalshi: verify your identity (KYC) and link a bank account or debit card.
- Polymarket: create a crypto wallet (MetaMask or Coinbase Wallet), buy USDC, send it to the wallet, then connect to Polymarket.

Do identity verification as early as possible — many people only find out at their first withdrawal that verification was needed, and wait around for days.

## Step 3: Read one resolution rule before choosing a market

This is the step beginners most often skip, and the most costly one.

Every market has a resolution rule that spells out exactly "what has to happen before it pays $1". For a "GDP growth above 3%" market, which agency and which release count? Those details hide in the fine print below.

**Before you order, if you can't say precisely "what makes it pay $1", you haven't understood the trade.** Pick a field you already know (economy, politics, sports, tech — anything), and read its rule through.

## Step 4: Place your first trade, $5–10 to start

The point of the first trade is to learn the interface, the fees, the spread and the fill process — not to profit.

- **Use a limit order, not a market order.** A limit order lets you set your own price and saves the spread; a market order eats someone else's quote and loses money the moment you enter.
- Place 1–5 contracts ($5–10 worth) — enough to walk the whole flow.

## Step 5: Watch, and learn to exit early

After your first trade, don't just wait for the outcome. Watch a few things:

- How does the price move with news?
- Is your position up or down right now?
- If price moves in your favour, can you sell to lock profit? If against, can you stomach cutting the loss?

One of prediction markets' biggest features is that **you don't have to hold to the end.** Learning to exit early is the dividing line between beginner and veteran.

## What this page should leave you with

1. US users start with Kalshi, overseas users with Polymarket — don't open both at once.
2. Fund $50–200 as tuition, and only money you can afford to lose.
3. Read the resolution rule through before you order; if you can't say "what pays $1", don't touch it.
4. First trade of $5–10 with a limit order, the goal being to walk the flow.
5. Learn to exit early — the single most important move for a beginner.
