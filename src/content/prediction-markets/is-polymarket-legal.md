"Is Polymarket legal?" has no one-line answer, because its compliance structure is layered: **it is, overall, an offshore crypto-native platform, with a separate US-regulated entity serving domestic access.** Which country and which state you're in yields a completely different answer.

## 1. Its relationship with the CFTC

Polymarket and the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) have a key history: the CFTC took action over how it operated event contracts, and Polymarket ultimately reached a settlement and paid a fine. The point here isn't that it was "banned" — it's that **it was named and penalized by a regulator, and continues operating within the regulator's sightline.**

This is different from "licensed operation." A settlement is neither a licence nor proof that every product is legal.

## 2. US users: state by state, not nationwide

Event contracts aren't a one-size-fits-all matter in the US. Polymarket's US entry **excludes some states** because their regulatory environments don't allow it. Which states are restricted shifts with policy and product, so this page deliberately avoids a fixed list that will go stale — **before you fund, check directly on the platform or in official announcements whether your state is supported.**

Key points:

- It's not a binary "legal in the US / illegal in the US" — it's "which states can, which states can't."
- The restricted-state list changes; don't treat an old article as current.

## 3. Overseas users: a different set of rules

If you're an overseas (non-US) user, you face Polymarket's offshore crypto platform, and the rules differ again:

- It's not directly under CFTC jurisdiction, but **it is governed by the laws of your own country/region.**
- Many jurisdictions have their own view of "event contracts / prediction markets / gambling" — some prohibit, some sit in a grey zone.
- Holding a balance from a restricted region may carry risk at review time.

**Bottom line: for overseas users, "can I use it" depends on your own country, not on the US.**

## 4. What to do before you fund

Wherever you are, check these four things before you fund — they avoid most of the traps:

1. **Is your country/state on the supported list** — go by the latest official announcement.
2. **Is the market you're betting compliant** — the same platform may treat different categories (elections, sports, crypto) differently under compliance.
3. **The money path** — a crypto-native platform means you manage the wallet and keys yourself; fund safety is on you.
4. **Does withdrawal work** — confirm your identity can smoothly convert USDC back into fiat.

## What this page should leave you with

1. Polymarket is a layered "offshore crypto platform + regulated US entry" — it can't be defined in one sentence.
2. It has settled with the CFTC and operates in the regulator's sightline, but that isn't full licensing.
3. US users look at "which state", not "the whole US"; the restricted-state list changes.
4. Overseas users look at "your country's law", independent of the US.
5. Checking your jurisdiction before funding is the only real bottom line.
