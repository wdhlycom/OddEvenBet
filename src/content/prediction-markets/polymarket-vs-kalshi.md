The most common question from beginners: Polymarket or Kalshi, which one do I pick?

The answer isn't "which is better" but "which fits you." These two take completely different paths. I'll lay out the differences first, then help you match.

## A table to see the difference

| | Polymarket | Kalshi |
|---|---|---|
| Regulation | Crypto-native, no US licence | CFTC-regulated (US) |
| On/off-ramp | USDC (a dollar-pegged stablecoin, via a crypto wallet) | Bank transfer, debit card |
| Market range | Politics, crypto, global events, sports | Economy, politics, weather, culture |
| Depth | Deepest in event contracts | Shallower than Polymarket |
| Fees | No trading fee — earns via the spread | Per contract, varies by market |
| Who it suits | Overseas, crypto-savvy, wants depth | US, wants regulation, wants fiat |

## The first dividing line: are you in the US?

This is the most important one, because it's not "preference" — it's "can you even use it."

- **Polymarket restricts US users.** Some work around it technically, but that violates the terms and can involve legal issues.
- **Kalshi is the legal, compliant US choice.**

So: **if you're in the US, stop deliberating — use Kalshi.** If you're overseas (or already use a crypto wallet), Polymarket is your main arena.

## The second dividing line: what are you betting?

The two markets lean in different directions:

- **Politics, macro, economic data** (Fed hikes, CPI, elections) — both have these, but Kalshi's economic-data contracts are especially strong, because its regulation lets it do many traditional-finance contracts Polymarket can't.
- **Sports, crypto, breaking global events** — Polymarket is far stronger. In Q1 2026, Polymarket's sports volume passed $10 billion, an order of magnitude Kalshi can't touch.

Simply: **betting economic data and macro, Kalshi is smoother; betting sports, crypto, global events, Polymarket is the only choice.**

## The third dividing line: what do you care about?

- **Regulation and fund safety**: pick Kalshi. There's a regulator you can name behind it — somewhere to appeal when something goes wrong.
- **Depth and speed**: pick Polymarket. Its order book depth is the deepest in the industry; large orders get in and out with little slippage, and settlement is instant (USDC lands within seconds).
- **"USD, no crypto"**: pick Kalshi. Fully fiat, no need to understand wallets or buy stablecoins.
- **"Lots of markets, bet anything"**: pick Polymarket.

## Advice from someone who's been there

Don't overthink this. **Pick one, get comfortable, then consider the second.**

- In the US → Kalshi, straight from your bank account, easiest.
- Overseas and crypto-savvy → Polymarket, the most complete and deepest market.
- Overseas but want to avoid crypto → honestly your options are limited; Polymarket is the most mature and is worth the half-hour to learn a wallet.

**Don't rush big money into either.** Fund $50–100 first, walk the full flow — open, deposit, first trade, withdraw — confirm it works for you, then talk about adding money.

## What this page should leave you with

1. Whether you're in the US is the first dividing line — it decides "can you use it", not "do you want to".
2. Betting economic/macro, pick Kalshi; betting sports/crypto, pick Polymarket.
3. Care about regulation, pick Kalshi; care about depth and speed, pick Polymarket.
4. Pick one, get comfortable, then fund small and walk the full flow.
5. Only stake money you can afford to lose — the same applies to both.
