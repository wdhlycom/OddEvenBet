## What Underdog Predict actually is

A prediction market bolted onto Underdog's daily fantasy platform, run on its own CFTC-regulated exchange. The defining feature is the Combo: you can join a prediction pick with a fantasy Higher/Lower line on a single entry.

That is the differentiator, and it is a real one. No other DFS operator in this list lets you cross prediction and fantasy lines on one ticket. It also means the audience is pre-built — millions of existing DFS users who do not have to learn a new product.

## Fees

This is the weak spot. Underdog advertises roughly $0.01 per contract each way, but adds exchange and regulatory fees whose exact size varies depending on where you read it. The all-in cost is genuinely hard to state cleanly, which is not a problem you should have to solve yourself.

The $10 minimum deposit is low, which lowers the barrier to entry but also makes it easy to lose small amounts through accumulated per-contract fees.

## Liquidity and markets

Underdog runs its own exchange after acquiring Aristotle in 2026, which should eventually mean better prices than renting a third-party book. The trade-off is track record: this exchange has less than a year of live operation, and there is no multi-year history of how it behaves in stressed markets.

## Licensing and coverage

CFTC-regulated, US-only, across roughly 30+ states — a list that notably includes California, Texas and Florida, three of the biggest DFS markets. That breadth is a genuine advantage over operators whose sports contracts shrink to a dozen or so states.

## Who this is wrong for

If you want to know your exact fee before you hit confirm, look elsewhere — the exchange and regulatory add-ons make the true cost murkier than it should be. If you are not already in the Underdog ecosystem, you are adopting a brand-new exchange with no track record to inspect.

If you are a fantasy pick'em player who already trusts the Underdog app and wants to fold predictions into the same habits, the Combo mechanic is the reason to try it.
