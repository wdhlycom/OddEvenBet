## What OG.com actually is

Crypto.com's event-contract market, launched in 2026, covering NBA, NFL, MLB, soccer, tennis, MMA, F1 and more. It leans social and casual: low stakes, follow-style features, and parlays from two to twenty legs. It runs through Crypto.com's CFTC-registered Derivatives North America, which is the meaningful regulatory anchor here.

## Fees

You pay roughly $0.02 per contract on each side of the trade. On a single small position that reads as trivial, but it compounds: frequent traders churning small contracts pay that fee on every entry and exit. ACH and wire deposits are free, while some express payment rails and instant withdrawals carry charges.

## Parlays and the honest math

The parlay is the emotional centerpiece and the mathematical trap. A two-to-twenty-leg parlay only pays if every leg lands; one miss and the entire stake is gone. That is a structurally negative-expectation bet no matter how the interface presents it. Treat it as entertainment, never as a system.

## Licensing

The federal license through Derivatives North America is the main reason to take this seriously versus offshore crypto venues. But the platform is still young, and the funds and tax boundaries with the parent company are still evolving — the kind of thing to watch, not hand-wave.

## Who this is wrong for

If your goal is positive expected value, the per-contract fees and parlay structure work against you. If you want a low-friction, casual, crypto-native way to take a view on sports you already follow, it does that job — just size it as entertainment.
