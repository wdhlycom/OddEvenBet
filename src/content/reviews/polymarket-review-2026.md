## What Polymarket actually is

An order book for binary event contracts, settled on-chain in USDC. There is no house taking the other side. Your counterparty is whoever sold you the contract, and the platform's role is matching and settlement.

That architecture is the source of both its strength and its risk. Strength: no bookmaker limiting your stake, no odds compression, no account closure for winning. Risk: when something breaks, there is no regulator to appeal to.

## Fees

Polymarket charges takers and not makers on most categories, with the fee calculated as a function of probability — highest near 50%, tapering toward 0% and 100%. In practice that means a market priced at 0.50 costs noticeably more to cross than one at 0.95.

For a market maker this is attractive: quote both sides, collect the spread, pay nothing for the privilege. For a taker who trades frequently in mid-priced markets, it adds up.

## Liquidity

This is the real differentiator. On major political and crypto markets, depth within two cents of mid routinely absorbs five-figure orders without moving the price. No competitor currently matches that on the same contracts.

The exception matters: niche markets — a specific court ruling, a corporate event — can carry wide spreads and thin depth. Do not assume that because the election market is liquid, the obscure market you found is too.

## Settlement and payout

Contracts settle to $1 or $0 in USDC. There is no withdrawal queue in the traditional sense: once resolution is final, the balance is a token you control.

That is genuinely better than a 3-to-5-day fiat withdrawal — but it also means the security of your funds depends on key management, not on a bank's deposit guarantee.

## Licensing and the honest caveat

Polymarket operates as a crypto-native venue offshore, with a separate US-regulated entity for domestic access. It is not a licensed sportsbook and does not present itself as one.

For readers in jurisdictions where event contracts are restricted, this is not a technicality — access can be blocked, and a balance held from a restricted location may be at risk on review. Check your own jurisdiction before funding anything.

## Who this is wrong for

If you want a phone number to call when a withdrawal is stuck, a regulator who publishes enforcement actions against your operator, or a straightforward fiat deposit, Polymarket is the wrong venue and no amount of liquidity fixes that.

If you want the best price on a political contract and you accept self-custody risk, it is still the reference market — with the caveat that its 2026 share loss to Kalshi is a real signal about where the volume is going.
