## What Robinhood Predict actually is

Yes/No event contracts traded inside the Robinhood app, executed through CFTC-regulated exchanges such as Kalshi and ForecastEx. Each contract settles at $1 if you are right and $0 if you are wrong — the same binary mechanics as the rest of this category.

The defining feature is integration. There is no separate prediction wallet, no new deposit, no new account. Your brokerage balance funds your trades, which is the whole reason this is appealing and the whole reason it is worth a second look.

## Fees

Robinhood charges a variable commission around $0.01 per contract plus exchange fees, and Gold members get up to 50% off. That is cheap in absolute terms, though the "variable" wording means the number on the ticket is not always identical.

Funding and withdrawals reuse the brokerage rails, so there is no separate fee for moving money in or out. That is a real convenience advantage over platforms that make you maintain a dedicated wallet.

## State restrictions

This is where the honesty matters. Coverage is cut by category, not just by state: some states can trade certain event types and not others, and some states get no sports contracts at all. What you see in the app is not what a reader in another state sees.

Check your own state before assuming the menu matches a friend's.

## The risk no one should skip

Event contracts are derivatives that can expire worthless, and here they sit inside an account you may use for actual investing. Losing your full stake on a prediction is a different emotional event when the money comes out of the same balance as your stock holdings. Keep the two ideas separate in your head even though Robinhood merges them on one screen.

## Who this is wrong for

If you want prediction money ring-fenced from your investments, this is the wrong structure. If your state blocks the categories you actually care about, the low fees do not matter.

If you are already a Robinhood user and want the lowest-friction way to trade event contracts, the integration is genuinely hard to beat.
