## What Novig actually is

A peer-to-peer order book for sports event contracts, not a bookmaker taking the other side. You are matched directly against other users, and the price is set by supply and demand. In practice that means the vig a sportsbook builds into every line is absent — the number you see is closer to the market's honest probability than the number a bookmaker wants to sell you.

## Fees

The free tier is real but bounded. Pre-match direct orders and maker orders carry no fee, and deposits and withdrawals are free with no minimum withdrawal threshold. The moment you move into live, futures or parlay trading, you pay a taker fee. If most of your activity is pre-match straight bets, this is genuinely cheaper than a bookmaker; if you live in the in-play screen, the savings shrink fast.

## Liquidity

This is where the no-vig promise meets reality. A bookmaker guarantees you a price and a fill; Novig only matches what other users are willing to trade. On mainstream markets depth is workable, but on anything niche — or in the thin window right before tip-off — the book can be shallow, and a meaningful order will move the price. You save on vig and pay in slippage and patience.

## Licensing and state risk

Novig holds a CFTC designated-contract-market license, a federal status rather than the state-by-state gambling permits that sportsbooks carry. That uniformity is a real advantage — one license, roughly 47 states plus Washington, DC. But Arizona, Michigan and Nevada are blocked, and state-level legal challenges remain live, so the coverage map could narrow further.

## Who this is wrong for

If you want instant fills at any size, or you live in Arizona, Michigan or Nevada, this is not your venue. If you mostly make pre-match straight bets and can accept thinner books, the no-vig pricing is a structural edge worth taking — as long as you treat the state risk as unsettled.
