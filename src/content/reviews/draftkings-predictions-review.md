## What DraftKings Predictions actually is

Yes/No event contracts inside the DraftKings app, running on DraftKings' own exchange, DKeX (Railbird). It is a separate product from DraftKings Sportsbook, with a separate wallet and separate state eligibility — not an extra tab bolted onto the sportsbook.

That split matters more than the branding. Your prediction funds sit apart from your sportsbook balance, which is good for clarity and bad for convenience. You will transfer between the two rather than drawing from one pot.

## Fees

Takers pay roughly $0.005–$0.01 per contract and makers roughly $0.0025. That is competitive against peers that charge a flat cent or more each way, and the maker rate in particular rewards limit orders.

There is no house edge baked into an odds line the way a traditional sportsbook works. You are paying per contract to trade, not paying vig on every settlement. That is cheaper for disciplined traders and more expensive for anyone who churns small positions.

## Liquidity and markets

Because DraftKings controls DKeX rather than renting a third-party book, it can promise depth on major markets. Yes/No contracts, combo markets and MLB player contracts are the core menu.

The honest caveat is geographic, not product. DraftKings can offer sports contracts in roughly 18 states without its own sportsbook footprint. In the other ~30 states plus DC where the platform operates, you are limited to whatever non-sports contracts are available.

## Licensing

DraftKings Predictions is CFTC-registered, which is the same federal frame the rest of this category operates under. That is a real backstop, not a Curacao license. It is still only available in the US, and eligibility follows the app's state-by-state rules.

## Who this is wrong for

If you want one combined balance across betting and predictions, the separate-wallet design will annoy you. If you live in a state where DraftKings sports contracts are not offered, the category is half-empty for you.

If you already hold a DraftKings account and want event contracts without new onboarding, the convenience is real — just accept that the unit is still a growth stage business spending to acquire users, and that a contract can expire worthless.
