You might wonder: a site you can read for free — what keeps it alive? This article lays it out plainly, nothing hidden.

## What an affiliate link is

In one sentence: an affiliate link is a URL "with a tag on it."

When you click that link to sign up for a platform, the platform knows "this user came from OddEvenBet." If you later actually open an account and deposit money, the platform pays us a commission as a "referral fee."

For you, clicking the link or not makes zero difference — the signup experience is identical, and the price is exactly the same. The only difference: the platform credits us for bringing you.

---

## How the commission works

Different platforms pay differently. Two common models:

- **One-time (CPA)**: you sign up and hit some condition (e.g. first deposit), and the platform pays us a fixed amount once.
- **Revenue share**: we get a percentage of your future activity on the platform.

Which model, and how much, varies by platform and by region.

---

## Does that money change what we write?

This is the most important question, and our answer is explicit: **no.**

Three reasons:

1. **Our recommendations have public criteria.** Every platform's score comes from a fixed scoring sheet in [our methodology](/methodology), not from a gut call.
2. **We don't charge for ratings.** No platform can buy a high score or a ranking slot from us.
3. **We write the downsides.** Every review must list at least one con. If a platform is genuinely bad, we'd rather earn nothing than push it.

If you ever find us recommending something that doesn't live up to it, tell us. Our reputation is worth more than any single commission.

---

## Where we disclose it

Everywhere an affiliate link appears, we mark it clearly. On every review and ranking you read, the page carries a notice along the lines of "this article contains affiliate links."

It's also a legal requirement in many countries — and we go further than the requirement.
