When a beginner bets, they think about "who will win." When a pro bets, they think about "did the market price this wrong." Those are two completely different games.

## The key difference: win rate vs value

"Predicting who wins" chases **win rate** — how often you're right. "Finding the underpriced" chases **value** — whether this bet's price is better than its true probability.

These aren't the same thing. Look at two extremes:

- **A person with a 70% win rate**, if they only ever bet on "favorites so hot there's almost no profit" (like -400 lines), can still lose long-term — because the money from winning 7 times doesn't cover the one upset that wipes them out.
- **A person with only a 50% win rate**, if they only bet when "the price is wrong and the odds are too generous," can profit long-term — because they win more when they win and lose less when they lose.

**Whether betting makes money doesn't depend on how often you're right — it depends on how much you win when right and how much you lose when wrong.**

## What value looks like

In one sentence: **when the market thinks something has a 60% chance but your research says it's really 70%, that bet has value.** You don't need it to actually happen — you just need "your judgment to be a little sharper than the market's," and then to place that bet repeatedly over the long run.

Which is why "finding value" is so much harder than "picking winners":

- To pick winners, you only need to be better than one opponent (the other team);
- To find value, you need to be better than **the whole market** — and the market is millions of people just like you, doing research and voting with real money.

So let's be honest: **most people can't find value, because they aren't actually sharper than the market.** That's not a knock on you — it's a warning not to overrate yourself.

## How to build an eye for value

Value isn't mysticism. There are a few concrete starting points:

1. **Only bet in fields you genuinely understand.** If you have an information advantage in a sport or league that others don't, only then are you qualified to talk about value. Chasing whatever's hot means you're always one step behind the market.

2. **Learn to convert odds to probability, then compare against your own judgment.** This is the implied probability from the odds article. The further your judgment is from the market's, the bigger the value — though of course, you might also be wrong.

3. **Watch how opening and closing lines move.** How a line moves from open to close reveals where big money is going. Over time, the people who can consistently "beat the close" are the ones actually winning.

4. **Keep honest records.** Not "how much I won," but "my estimated probability vs what actually happened" — so you can see how accurate your judgment really is. Most people go their whole lives without doing this, which is why they never realize they're just running on luck.

## What this page should leave you with

1. Betting isn't about "who wins" — it's about "did the market price this wrong."
2. A 50% win rate can profit and a 70% can lose; the difference is in how much you win and lose per outcome.
3. Finding value = your judgment is a little sharper than the market's, repeated over the long run.
4. Don't overrate yourself: most people can't find value because they aren't sharper than the market.
5. Only bet in fields you truly understand, learn to convert odds to probability, and keep honest records.
6. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
