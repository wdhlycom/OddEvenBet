Some people blame all their betting wins and losses on "how accurate their judgment is." But there's a more basic question most people overlook: **how much do you bet per trade?** Judgment can be excellent, but if you size a trade too large, one losing streak clears you out of the game. That's exactly what <details class="term"><summary>bankroll management</summary><span class="term-note">Treating the money you bet with as a fixed pool and strictly controlling what fraction of it each bet takes. Its purpose is to keep you in the game long-term.</span></details> is for.

## The core, in one line: bet only a small slice each time

Treat your bankroll as a fixed pool. **Bet only 1% to 3% of that pool per trade.**

For example: a $1,000 bankroll at 1–3% means $10 to $30 per bet.

That percentage feels tiny — almost unsatisfying. But its job isn't to make you rich fast; it's to keep you **around**. Mathematically, if you bet 1% per trade, even 30 straight losses leaves you with over 70% of your bankroll; if you bet 20% per trade, five straight losses halves it.

**Your bet size is one of the few variables you fully control.** Whether your judgment is right has a luck component — but "how much to bet" doesn't. It's pure discipline.

## Why use a "pool" instead of betting whatever you have

A lot of people lose money not from bad judgment but from **bet sizing that follows emotion**:

- You win, feel hot, and raise the next bet;
- You lose, want to claw it back, and double the next bet.

Both are using "feeling" to decide bet size — and feeling is the least reliable thing there is.

By carving your bankroll into a separate pool (strictly for betting, completely separate from living expenses), you give yourself an objective line. When the pool runs dry, you stop — no matter how "certain" the opportunity feels right now.

## The most dangerous one: never chase losses with bigger bets

This line deserves its own section, because it's the single most broken rule in bankroll management.

After a loss, your brain fires off a fierce urge: "bet bigger on the next one and win back what I lost." That urge has a name — <details class="term"><summary>chasing losses</summary><span class="term-note">Increasing your bet after a loss to try to win back the money you lost. It's the most common and most lethal mistake among gamblers, traders, and investors alike.</span></details>. It almost always works once, which tricks you into thinking it "works," and then it swallows you completely on the next, bigger loss.

**Remember: money already lost is a sunk cost, and it has nothing to do with how much your next bet should be.** Your next bet size should be decided only by "how much of the pool is left," never by "how much I just lost."

## What this page should leave you with

1. Bet only 1–3% of your bankroll per trade; that percentage lets you survive long losing runs.
2. Bet size is one of the few variables you fully control — don't hand it to emotion.
3. Keep your bankroll in a separate pool, apart from living expenses.
4. Never chase losses with bigger bets — it's the most broken and most lethal rule in bankroll management.
5. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
