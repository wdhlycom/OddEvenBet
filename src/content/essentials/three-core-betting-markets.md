Sports betting has an endless menu of bets, but almost all of them sit on top of three core markets. Master these three and you'll instantly see which foundation any exotic bet is decorating.

## One — the moneyline

**The simplest bet there is: just pick who wins.** No margin, no spread — win and you win, lose and you lose.

- Lakers -180 vs Celtics +155: bet $180 on the Lakers to win $100; bet $100 on the Celtics to win $155.

The moneyline suits **low-scoring sports** — baseball, hockey, soccer — because those games have small margins, which makes a spread awkward. Its trap: **the favorite's odds are often ugly** (-300, -400), so you stake a lot to win a little, and one upset wipes out a chunk of progress.

## Two — the point spread

**Subtract points from the favorite, add points to the underdog, and turn a lopsided matchup into a near-50/50 bet.**

- If the Chiefs are -6.5 vs the Broncos: bet the Chiefs and they must win by **7 or more** for you to win; bet the Broncos and they must lose by 6 or fewer (or win outright) for you to win.

That "-6.5" is the <details class="term"><summary>point spread</summary><span class="term-note">The handicap the book gives the favorite to level the field. Spreads with a .5 (like -6.5) always produce a winner; whole-number spreads (like -7) can push and refund.</span></details>. The spread suits **high-scoring sports** — football, basketball — because they score enough that a few points actually matter.

The spread's trap: **half-point margins are the norm.** Countless games turn on that half point, which is what makes it thrilling and torturous in equal measure.

## Three — the over/under

**Not betting who wins — betting whether the two teams' combined score goes over or under a number.**

- An NFL total set at 47.5: if you think the teams combine for 48 or more, bet the "over"; if 47 or fewer, bet the "under."

The over/under's biggest virtue: **you don't have to pick a side.** Even if you're unsure who wins, if you have a judgment about *how the game will play out in terms of pace*, you can bet. It works for every sport.

Its trap: **the total is easily hijacked by pace.** A game that should be a slow defensive grind can suddenly trade blows and send the total soaring. Betting over/under is really betting on "the style of the game," not the winner.

## All three side by side

| Market | What you're betting | Suited sports | Main trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moneyline | Who wins | Baseball, hockey, soccer | Ugly favorite odds, big loss on an upset |
| Point spread | Win by how much | Football, basketball | Half-point margins decide it |
| Over/under | Combined score | Every sport | Hijacked by game pace |

**Advice for beginners**: start with the moneyline and the over/under. The moneyline is the most intuitive; the over/under doesn't require picking a side. Hold off on the point spread until you understand "how far apart the two teams really are" — otherwise you're guessing blind.

## What this page should leave you with

1. Moneyline bets who wins (suits low-scoring sports); the spread bets by how much (high-scoring); over/under bets the combined score.
2. The spread's trap is half-point margins deciding the result; the over/under's trap is getting hijacked by pace.
3. Beginners should start with moneyline and over/under, and touch the spread only once they understand the real gap between teams.
4. These three are the foundation every fancy bet is built on.
5. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
