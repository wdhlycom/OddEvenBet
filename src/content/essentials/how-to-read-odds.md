The first thing you see on any bet is the odds. They do two jobs at once: **tell you how likely something is, and how much you'll win if it hits.** The same odds are written three ways around the world, same wine, different bottle. Reading them is the first skill you must have before betting.

## Three formats, one thing

### American odds

Most common in the US, written as a signed number.

- **Negative (-150)**: this is the favorite. It reads "you must stake $150 to win $100."
- **Positive (+200)**: this is the underdog. It reads "you stake $100 to win $200."

The memory aid: **a minus sign tells you "how much to stake to win 100," a plus sign tells you "how much you win on a 100 stake."**

### Decimal odds

Most common in Europe and Australia — a decimal above 1 that directly says "how much you get back per $1 staked (including your stake)."

- Odds of 2.50: stake $1, and if you win you get back $2.50 — $1.50 of that is profit, $1 is your stake.

Decimal odds are the easiest, because one multiplication gives you the total return.

### Fractional odds

Most common in the UK, written as a fraction.

- Odds of 3/1: stake $1, win $3 in profit (plus your $1 stake back).

"3/1" is read "three to one" — the numerator is profit, the denominator is your stake.

## The core skill: converting odds to probability

Behind every set of odds hides an <details class="term"><summary>implied probability</summary><span class="term-note">The probability of an event as reverse-engineered from the odds. It's never the true probability — because the book has taken vig out of it, it runs slightly high. But it lets you compare different odds side by side.</span></details>. Learn the conversion and you can judge "is this bet actually worth it?"

A few examples (on a $100 stake):

| Odds format | Profit if you win | Implied probability |
|---|---|---|
| American -150 | $66.67 | 60% |
| American +200 | $200 | 33% |
| Decimal 2.50 | $150 | 40% |
| Fractional 3/1 | $300 | 25% |

**How to read this table**: odds of -150 mean the market thinks this is about 60% likely. The bigger the positive number, the lower the probability — but the more you win when it hits.

## The book's profit is hidden in the odds

Whatever the format, odds are never a "fair price." The book hides a little profit in every price, called the <details class="term"><summary>vig</summary><span class="term-note">vig or margin — the profit the book takes from every bet. A standard market is usually 4–5%, and the fancier the product (parlays, props), the harder the take.</span></details>.

For example, a truly 50/50 game should have fair odds of +100 on both sides (stake 100, win 100). But the book usually posts -110 on both sides — you must stake $110 to win $100. That extra slice is the book's profit.

So **odds tell you what the market thinks, not what the truth is.** Reading odds is only the first step; the real work is judging whether the market got it wrong.

## What this page should leave you with

1. Odds express both "probability" and "payout" — the three formats (American/decimal/fractional) are the same thing.
2. American: negative means "how much to stake to win 100," positive means "how much you win on 100."
3. The core skill is converting odds to implied probability, then judging whether a bet is worth it.
4. Every set of odds hides the book's vig — roughly 4–5% on a standard market.
5. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
