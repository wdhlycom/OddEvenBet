Opening this site for the first time, you might feel a little lost: so many articles, which do I read first?

This article is your map. You don't need to read everything — just follow the line below, and you'll spend the least time for the most ground covered.

---

## Step one: understand what a prediction market is

Before you touch any real money, spend ten minutes on the most basic question: **what exactly is a prediction market.**

- It isn't a casino, and there's no bookmaker standing against you.
- It's a contract system — you and someone else agree on whether something "will or won't happen."
- The contract's price (say $0.65) is the market's estimate of how likely it is.

Read this: [What is a prediction market](/essentials/what-is-a-prediction-market).

---

## Step two: understand "price" and "cost"

Once you know what it is, nail down two things, because they decide whether you can actually make money:

1. **How prices are set** — the price isn't the platform guessing; it's matched out of the order book.
2. **How fees and the spread eat your money** — this is the hidden reason most people lose.

Corresponding reads:

- [How prices are set](/essentials/how-prediction-market-prices-are-set)
- [How fees work](/essentials/how-prediction-market-fees-work)

---

## Step three: understand why most people lose

This step is critical, and counterintuitive.

Most people lose not because they're dumb or unlucky, but because **fees and spreads exceed their edge**. And "high win rate" doesn't equal "making money" — a distinction many people never grasp in their whole lives.

Read this: [Why most traders lose money](/essentials/why-most-traders-lose-money).

---

## Step four: decide whether you can afford to lose

Before touching real money, ask yourself one final question:

> If I lose all of this, can I still sleep tonight?

If yes, keep going; if no, stop here.

Read this: [Why you should only bet money you can afford to lose](/essentials/only-bet-what-you-can-afford-to-lose).

---

## After you finish the line

If you've walked all four steps, you're already ahead of most people who walk in — because most people charge in without researching a single thing.

Next, if you want to actually look at platforms, move on to the reviews and comparisons. But don't reverse the order: **understand the principles first, then the platforms, then touch money last.**

---

## In one sentence

> Understand what it is → understand price and cost → face why people lose → decide what you can afford to lose.

Finish this line and you'll have the confidence not to pay tuition.
