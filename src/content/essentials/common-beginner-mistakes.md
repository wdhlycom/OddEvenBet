Beginners lose money not because their judgment is bad, but because they step into a few traps that "look fine but are actually lethal." This article walks through each one. Check yourself against this list before every trade and you'll pay far less tuition.

---

## Trap one: treating a high price as "safe"

A $0.95 contract looks almost like a lock. But do the math:

- If it wins, you make $0.05 (five cents).
- If it loses, you lose the full $0.95.

And a $0.95 price means it still has a 5% chance of going to zero. Five times out of a hundred, you lose everything.

So remember: **a high price doesn't mean safe — it just means "more likely to win, but you win less when it does."** The real math runs on expected value, not on "will it happen or not."

---

## Trap two: treating volume as liquidity

A market trading actively doesn't mean the spread is narrow at the exact moment you enter.

Volume is "how many people traded in the past." Liquidity is "can you get a good fill right now." Those are two different things.

Before you enter, look at the **current spread** and the **orders sitting near the current price** — not the trade history. A quiet market, even if it traded heavily a few days ago, may now have only one market maker posting a wide spread.

---

## Trap three: forgetting you can exit early

A lot of people assume that once you buy a contract you have to hold it to settlement. You don't.

In a prediction market you can sell your contract at any time. If the price moves your way, you can sell and lock in a profit — the event never has to resolve.

This matters because it's the reason prices react to news quickly, and it's your "take profit" tool. Don't stubbornly hold all the way to the end and miss the exit window.

---

## Trap four: not reading the settlement rules

Every market has a written settlement rule, usually buried in the fine print. It defines precisely "what counts as the event happening."

"A team wins" and "a team wins in 90 minutes" are two different things, and they can settle completely differently.

**Read the rule before you trade.** If you can't say "what exactly must happen for this to pay $1," you don't actually understand the trade.

---

## Trap five: betting money you can't afford to lose

This is the most lethal one, which is why we repeat it on its own.

Prediction markets are an arena of judgment, but they punish mistakes without mercy. If the money you stake would hurt your life to lose, your mindset breaks — and once your mindset breaks, your judgment follows.

Remember the iron rule: **only stake money you can afford to lose.** This isn't timidity; it's the precondition for staying in the game long-term.

---

## A checklist

Before every trade, ask yourself these five questions:

1. Do I know exactly how much I lose if it misses?
2. What's the current spread, as a percentage of the price?
3. Can I exit early, and what does exiting cost?
4. Did I read the settlement rule, and can I say what must happen for it to pay?
5. Is this money I can afford to lose?

If you can answer all five, place the trade. If not, pause.
