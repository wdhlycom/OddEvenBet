There's one number that sets the tone for this entire industry: in prediction markets, the large majority of participant accounts lose money, net.

This isn't a secret, and it isn't because these people are stupid. It's because the structure is tilted in a direction most people never see.

---

## A high win rate is a fake feeling

The most dangerous metric in this world is the **win rate**. Why? Look at this example.

You buy ten "Yes" contracts, each a "90% chalk," at $0.90 each. Nine of them win — 90% win rate, sounds impressive, right?

But do the math:

- Nine wins, $0.10 each = **+$0.90**
- One loss, $0.90 = **−$0.90**

You broke even — **on a 90% win rate.**

And that's before fees and spreads. Add those in and you're straight into the red.

So remember: **in a market with asymmetric payouts, win rate has nothing to do with making money.** Most people who brag about their win rate are losing money and just haven't noticed.

---

## Where the money actually goes

Every buy and sell stacks three costs together:

1. **The spread.** You buy at the "ask" and sell at the "bid" — a few percent vanish in the round trip.
2. **Fees.** Taking liquidity costs a fee, and it's highest right around 50% probability — which is exactly where retail loves to trade.
3. **You're facing informed people.** When you get filled, the person on the other side often knows something you don't. You are systematically taking the "smarter" side of the trade.

All three add up to this: an ordinary trader needs a genuine probability **edge of over 5%** just to break even.

Most people neither have that edge, nor know they need it.

---

## Who is actually making money

The people who make money consistently fall into a few specific groups, and the "smart amateur" isn't one of them:

- **Market makers**: they earn the spread and rebates, not by predicting outcomes.
- **Speed arbitrageurs**: they correct cross-platform price gaps within a second.
- **Experts**: people with real information advantages in one narrow field — a specific court system, a specific league.

Note who's missing: the people who are "great at predicting the news." That's not a job here, because everyone has the news.

---

## An honest reminder

The question isn't "can you make money in prediction markets" — for the groups above, yes. The question is "which group am I in."

If the answer is "none of them," your result is the same as most people's: a slow bleed disguised as a string of "almost won" moments.

So before you put real money in, figure out which side you're on. Which is also why we keep repeating: **only stake money you can afford to lose.**
