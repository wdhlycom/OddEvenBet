Here's a brutal fact about betting: **most people lose not because their judgment is poor, but because their emotions drag them off a cliff.** Judgment can be learned from a book; the emotional hurdle is the real divider between who survives and who doesn't. This article skips the pep talk and goes straight to three common emotional traps plus a few concrete rules that actually work.

## Trap one: chasing losses

After a loss, your brain desperately wants to "go again and win it back." We covered this in the bankroll management article — it's the single most lethal one.

Why is it so dangerous? Because **it almost always works once.** You raise your stake, get lucky, and claw it back — and your brain files it away as "a method that works." Then next time, you repeat it on a bigger loss, until you're fully out of the game.

**The fix**: set yourself an iron rule — **any new bet is judged only by "how much of the pool is left," never by "how much I just lost."** Lost money is in the past, and it has nothing to do with how you should bet the future.

## Trap two: FOMO

You see a hot event everyone's piling into and you're afraid of missing out, so you pile in too. Or a team wins several in a row, so you think "it'll keep winning" and chase it.

This fear-of-missing-out mindset gets you in right when the market has already pushed the price to its peak. **You're not chasing value — you're chasing emotion.**

**The fix**: ask yourself one question — "if I weren't currently paying attention to this, would I still place this bet?" If the answer is no, it's emotion-driven. Don't place it.

## Trap three: overconfidence

After a few wins in a row, people get cocky — "I've found my touch" — and start raising stakes, widening their scope, and touching fields they don't understand.

Overconfidence when you're winning is more insidious than chasing losses when you're losing, because it wears the costume of "I'm in form." But it's just as lethal: **your bets get bigger, but your judgment didn't actually get better.**

**The fix**: write your stake size down as a rule (e.g. always 1–3%) and let the rule stop you, instead of letting "how good I feel right now" decide how much to bet.

## Control emotions with rules, not willpower

The three fixes above are really the same thing: **don't make decisions when emotions are at their hottest — write the rules in advance.**

A few copy-paste-ready rules:

1. **Write it down before you bet**: why you're placing this bet, what the logic is. Look back when emotions run hot and you'll usually find the logic doesn't hold up.
2. **Set a stopping point**: e.g. "lose 5 bets in a row and I stop for the day" or "lose X% of the bankroll and I stop." When you hit it, stop — no matter how itchy your hands get.
3. **Don't bet during high emotional volatility**: right after a loss, right after an argument, or after drinking — none of these are trading conditions.

## What this page should leave you with

1. Three common emotional traps: chasing losses, FOMO, and overconfidence.
2. Chasing losses is the most dangerous because it almost always works once — then swallows you the next time.
3. FOMO is "fear of missing out" — it gets you in right when the market has pushed the price to its peak.
4. Overconfidence makes you raise your stakes after wins, but your judgment didn't actually improve.
5. Managing emotions is done through rules written in advance, not willpower.
6. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
