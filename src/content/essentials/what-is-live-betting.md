Most bets are locked in before kickoff. But there's a kind you can keep placing after the game has started, even midway — that's <details class="term"><summary>live betting</summary><span class="term-note">live betting / in-play betting — placing bets in real time while a game is underway, with odds refreshing constantly against the score, pace, and events.</span></details>.

## How live betting works

The moment a game starts, the book continuously re-rates its odds based on what's happening:

- A team scores first and its moneyline odds immediately shorten (it's more likely to win now);
- A game plays slow and the over/under line may drift down;
- A key player goes down injured and the whole board gets re-priced.

The price you see is **the market's judgment at this moment**, and it can change in the next second. That's the biggest difference from pre-game betting: **pre-game lines are static; live lines are alive and always moving.**

## Why it's a disaster zone for beginners

The appeal of live betting is obvious: halfway through, the situation looks clearer, so it feels "easier to judge." But that appeal is exactly the trap. Three reasons:

1. **You make decisions at your most emotional moment.** Watching a game is when people get most fired up. The moment your team takes a lead you rush to chase it, the moment it falls behind you want to claw back — judgments made in that state are far worse than calm ones.

2. **The price has already eaten your information.** You think you "saw the situation," but everything you can see (who scored, who got hurt) the book saw faster and more completely, and it's already priced in. By the time you react, the value is usually gone.

3. **Time pressure.** Pre-game you can study over three days; live betting forces you to decide in seconds. Under time pressure, error rates shoot up.

## When it's worth touching live betting

Live betting isn't unplayable, but it has a bar. Wait until you can do the following before touching it:

1. **You've already mastered pre-game lines.** Get many pre-game calls right calmly first, before considering in-play.
2. **You have a plan set before the game.** E.g. "if Team X is down 10 or less at halftime, and I already judged their second half is strong, I bet." The plan triggers the bet, not emotion.
3. **You can accept not chasing.** The most dangerous move is immediately raising your stake to claw back a loss. Live betting is especially good at baiting this move, because of the illusion that "the next goal is coming any second."

## What this page should leave you with

1. Live betting is betting in real time, with odds shifting against the game state.
2. Its trap: you decide at your most emotional, most time-pressed moment, while the book has already priced in everything you can see.
3. Beginners should master pre-game lines first, then touch live betting only with a plan set before the game.
4. Never raise your stake to claw back a loss — live betting makes that especially tempting.
5. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
