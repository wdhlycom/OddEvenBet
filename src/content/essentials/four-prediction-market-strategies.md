We referenced earlier that most people in prediction markets lose money. But among the losers, there's a group that isn't "wrong about the outcome" — they're "bad at executing." They place a bet on a feeling, credit themselves when it wins, and blame luck when it loses. This article breaks down the core strategies that experienced traders abroad use again and again, to move you from "gut feel" to "method."

## Strategy one: news trading

This is the most intuitive one. The logic is simple: when news the market hasn't absorbed yet breaks, the price moves, and the fastest, most accurate judge makes money.

- A poll shifts hard, a policy leaks, a ruling drops — the price jumps within minutes.
- The key isn't being "fast;" it's judging "how much this news is actually worth."

**But there's a brutal caveat**: by the time you read the news on a big site, the market has usually already moved. So this strategy really rewards people who *already* follow the field long-term — you have first-hand judgment on polls, policy, or matchups, so the news isn't "new information" to you; it's "confirmation."

## Strategy two: fading the crowd

Markets get emotional — they swing between panic and euphoria.

- A candidate gets hit with a scandal and the contract price crashes from $0.50 to $0.10. The question to ask: at 10%, has the market over-panicked?
- A "sure thing" favorite gets bid up to $0.90. The question: at 90%, has the market over-euphorized?

Fading the crowd means **standing on the other side of a crowd at emotional extremes**. But it demands real conviction and patience — after you buy in, the price can keep falling, and you have to ride out the "unrealized loss" until the facts pull the price back.

**The biggest trap with fading the crowd**: it's not that you're always wrong — it's that you can't stomach the unrealized loss and cut early. So this strategy only suits people who have truly thought it through and whose position is small enough not to cost them sleep.

## Strategy three: diversification

Don't put your whole bankroll on a single event. This is the simplest rule and the most underrated.

- Spread your capital across a few uncorrelated events — some politics, some economics, some sports.
- On each one, only bet the few spots where your judgment has an edge.

The point isn't the empty phrase "spreading risk." It's this: **your edge pays off across many trades, never through one.** A single result can be luck; many results are skill. Diversification forces you into the "many trades" game.

## Strategy four: limit orders to cut costs

This is the most "technical" — and the most practical — move.

Placing a market order means directly eating someone else's listed price, paying the spread the moment you enter; placing a limit order means posting your own price and waiting for someone to fill it — the spread is yours.

- On a $0.50 contract, a 2-cent spread means a market order loses 4% on entry.
- A limit order 1 cent lower, even if you wait a little, saves that 4%.

**Over a hundred trades, this one habit alone can be a difference of thousands of dollars.** And it has nothing to do with your judgment — it's pure execution discipline.

## Pick one and master it first

These four aren't a menu to do all at once. The most common beginner mistake is "trying all of them" and ending up mastering none.

**Pick one and master it.** My suggested order:

1. Start with **limit orders** — it's a pure habit, independent of judgment, and it saves money immediately.
2. Then **diversification** — it forces you into the "many trades" game instead of going all-in.
3. Only then **news trading** or **fading the crowd** — these depend on real judgment and need to be built up slowly.

## What this page should leave you with

1. Four core strategies: news trading, fading the crowd, diversification, and limit orders.
2. Beginners should master limit orders and diversification first, before touching the judgment-dependent strategies.
3. News trading rewards people who already understand the field, not people who read fast.
4. The hardest part of fading the crowd is sitting through unrealized losses — keep the position small.
5. Only stake money you can afford to lose — however good the method, any single result is still uncertain.
