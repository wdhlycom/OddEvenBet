One word separates a beginner from a pro: **expected value**.

A beginner asks "will this happen?" A pro computes "on average, over the long run, how much does this bet make me?"

This article explains expected value in the plainest words possible.

---

## Start with an example

There's a game with simple rules:

- Flip a coin. Heads, you win $200; tails, you lose $100.
- Each play costs $100.

Would you play?

A lot of people hesitate, because "there's a 50% chance of losing." But compute the expected value:

- Heads (50% chance): win $200
- Tails (50% chance): lose $100

Average per play = 0.5 × 200 − 0.5 × 100 = **+$50**.

That is, every time you play, you make $50 on average. A single play can lose, but over a hundred or a thousand plays, you'll consistently win.

**That's expected value: how much a bet makes (or loses) on average over the long run.**

---

## Why it matters more than win rate

Another example:

You buy a contract with a 90% chance to win; if it wins, you make $0.10; if it loses (10%), you lose $0.90.

The win rate is a lovely 90%. But compute the expected value:

- 0.9 × 0.10 − 0.1 × 0.90 = 0.09 − 0.09 = **0**

Expected value is zero. You make nothing — on a 90% win rate.

So remember: **win rate lies; expected value tells the truth.** A pro doesn't ask "how often did I win" — they ask "on average, how much did I make per bet."

---

## How to compute expected value

The formula is one line:

> **Expected value = (probability of winning × amount won) − (probability of losing × amount lost)**

If the result is positive, doing it long-term makes you money; if negative, doing it long-term loses you money.

That's all. The hard part isn't the formula — it's **honestly estimating those two probabilities and those two amounts.**

---

## The key: your judgment vs the market's price

In a prediction market, the core of making money is one line:

> **You have positive expected value only when "the true probability you believe" is higher than "the market price."**

The market prices it at $0.65, and you believe the true probability is 75% — then you have a 10-point edge, and it's worth betting.

If the market prices it at $0.65 and deep down you also think it's 65%, you have no edge, and betting just donates fees.

Most people skip this step — they don't estimate the "true probability"; they just feel "this is definitely going to happen." Which is exactly why most people lose.

---

## An honest reminder

Expected value is a "long-run" concept. It means:

- You can have positive EV and still lose ugly on any single bet.
- You can have negative EV and still win big on any single bet.

The real test is whether you can stomach the single outcomes and keep doing the positive-EV thing.

And whether you can keep going comes back to that iron rule: **only stake money you can afford to lose.** Money you can afford to lose, you can hold; and if you can hold, EV gets the chance to pay off.
