Major sports are the main battleground of betting: deep markets, full information, good liquidity — but your opponents are also the most professional, and the room to profit from an information edge is the smallest. Each of the five sports below has its own beginner page; first look at how they are positioned, then click through.

## Soccer

The world's largest betting market, with markets from the handicap and totals to corners and cards. Suits people who like studying league styles; the key variable is the draw — a perennially undervalued result.

[Soccer guide](/major-sports/soccer-betting-guide)

## NFL (American football)

The most transparent data in sports, built on three markets: the spread, totals and moneyline. Suits people willing to do fine-grained research; the key variable is the injury report.

[NFL guide](/major-sports/nfl-betting-guide)

## NBA (basketball)

Fast pace and many games, with mature spread, totals, moneyline and live betting. Suits people who like watching live; the key variables are back-to-backs and load management.

[NBA guide](/major-sports/nba-betting-guide)

## Hockey (NHL)

North America's largest yet most overlooked market, with spreads almost always set at 1.5 goals and a high upset rate. The key variables are the goaltender and back-to-backs.

[Hockey guide](/major-sports/nhl-betting-guide)

## MLB (baseball)

A distinctive odds structure built on the moneyline and the run line (usually 1.5 runs). Suits people willing to dig into the odds structure; the key variable is the starting pitcher.

[MLB guide](/major-sports/mlb-betting-guide)

Click through all five once, pick one to go deep on, and do not spread yourself too thin. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
