Hockey (NHL) is the most overlooked of North America's four major professional leagues among casual bettors. Yet it is actually one of the largest betting markets in the world — in the first quarter of 2026, hockey volume on Polymarket reached $690 million, ahead of tennis and American football.

For players in the English-speaking market, hockey is a good "mainstream, high-volume, but watched by fewer people than soccer or basketball" option. This page first introduces the sport, then explains how to bet it.

## Understand the season first: when there are games

The NHL's yearly rhythm is clean, split into four parts:

- **Regular season (October to the following April)**: each team plays 82 games, with games almost every day. This is the densest — and most overlooked — phase for betting.
- **Playoffs (mid-April to June)**: 16 teams play a knockout bracket, in a <details class="term"><summary>best-of-seven</summary><span class="term-note">Two teams play a series; the first to win 4 games advances. You need to win 16 games to lift the Cup, so there is almost no luck involved.</span></details> format. It is one of the hardest championships to win anywhere — you have to win 16 games to lift the Cup, with no single-game flukes.
- **The <details class="term"><summary>Stanley Cup</summary><span class="term-note">The NHL championship trophy, hockey's highest honor, decided in June.</span></details> final (June)**: the Eastern and Western champions meet, which is the peak of the whole season and the two hottest weeks for betting.
- **Off-season (June to September)**: trades and drafts, no games. Hockey markets go mostly quiet over these months.

**Advice for beginners: do not fixate on the June final.** The real value is in the regular season — games are dense, upsets are frequent, and most bettors are watching soccer and basketball, so hockey odds are more likely to be mispriced.

## Who the real contenders are (with some story to remember)

Hockey's best teams are not famous by name; they are defined by a stable system. These are the ones most worth following for the 2026-27 season:

- **Edmonton Oilers**: they have Connor McDavid — widely accepted as the best player in the world today — plus partner Leon Draisaitl, and their attacking firepower is historic. Their story is "always strong, still missing a title," so they are favored every year and the odds sit near the top. Betting them to win is not highly priced, but betting them to win the Cup is a perennial favorite.
- **Carolina Hurricanes**: the 2026 defending champions, built not on stars but on discipline and defense. They are the classic "no superstar, yet hardest to beat," especially hard to handle in the playoffs.
- **Colorado Avalanche**: the Nathan MacKinnon + Cale Makar pairing, one of the strongest offensive cores plus one of the strongest defensemen, with speed and a devastating attack. The catch is they are strong in the regular season but injury-prone in the playoffs — health is their only variable.
- **Florida Panthers**: they grind opponents down with fierce forechecking and airtight defense. They are not the prettiest team, but in a best-of-seven playoff, this style wears opponents out.
- **Vegas Golden Knights**: home-ice advantage is among the most fearsome in the league — visiting teams are beaten before they even take the ice.

**A one-line way to remember**: the Oilers are the "offensive ceiling," the Hurricanes and Panthers the "hardest playoff outs," the Avalanche "best in the regular season but health-dependent," and the Golden Knights "unbeatable at home."

## Three core markets

Hockey, like basketball, runs on three main markets:

- **Moneyline**: who wins, including overtime and penalty shootouts.
- **The spread (<details class="term"><summary>puck line</summary><span class="term-note">Hockey's name for the point spread, almost always set at 1.5 goals. Because hockey is low-scoring, 1.5 goals is already a large margin.</span></details>, usually 1.5 goals)**: hockey is low-scoring, so 1.5 goals is already a large handicap.
- **Over/under (totals)**: total goals scored, with the most common lines at 5.5 or 6 goals.

**The key difference**: hockey is low-scoring — games often produce only 3-5 goals. So a "1.5 goal" spread means the favorite must win by 2 goals for you to win — a large margin in hockey, and the upset rate is far higher than a basketball spread.

There are also two hockey-specific things to know:

- **The 60-minute line (three-way)**: only counts regulation time (60 minutes), so you can bet on a "draw." About one in five hockey games is tied after regulation, and many beginners do not realize whether they are on "with overtime" or "without overtime" — then they win in overtime but lose their money. **Before placing a bet, check whether it is moneyline or the 60-minute line.**
- **<details class="term"><summary>empty-net goal</summary><span class="term-note">The trailing team pulls its goaltender in the final minutes and adds an extra attacker; the opponent then scores into the empty net, turning a 1-goal lead into 2. This directly rewrites many spread and totals results.</span></details>**: the team behind pulls its goaltender and adds an attacker in the final minutes, then often concedes an empty-net goal, turning a one-goal lead into two. This directly decides many spread and totals outcomes.

## The most important, most overlooked variable: the goaltender

The biggest difference between hockey and other team sports is that **the goaltender has an enormous effect on the game, yet beginners barely look at it**.

- A hot top-tier goaltender can stop 40+ shots in a game and effectively carry the team to a win.
- When a starting goaltender rests and a backup comes in, the whole team's defensive level drops noticeably.

**Before you bet, check who is in net today.** A resting or out-of-form starting goaltender matters more than any other piece of team information. This is the single best-value action in hockey betting.

## The second key variable: back-to-back schedules

The NHL schedule is extremely dense, and teams often play "back-to-back" on consecutive days.

- On the second day of a back-to-back, a team's energy drops clearly and the upset rate rises significantly.
- Especially the "away team on a back-to-back" — flying in after an away game for the next one, in the worst condition.

**Back-to-backs are hockey's biggest source of upsets.** If you see a strong team playing the second game of a back-to-back, be extra cautious even if it is the better side.

## The unique logic of the totals market

Because hockey is low-scoring, the logic of the totals market differs from other sports:

- A 5.5-goal line means 3 goals vs 3, or 4 goals vs 2.
- Two defensive teams with strong goaltenders tend to produce "unders" (few total goals).
- Two attacking teams with loose defense tend to produce "overs."

Hockey totals are essentially a judgment of "are these teams defensive or attacking," plus "what shape are the two goaltenders in today."

## Common mistakes

- Not checking who is in net today and betting on general impressions.
- Ignoring back-to-backs and treating a strong team on the second leg as steady as usual.
- Confusing moneyline (with overtime) and the 60-minute line (without overtime), winning in overtime but losing the bet.
- Applying basketball logic to totals, ignoring hockey's low-scoring, low-score premise.

## What this page should leave you with

1. The NHL season runs from October to June, and the regular season is the highest-value phase to bet — do not just watch the final.
2. Contender cheat-sheet: Oilers "offensive ceiling," Hurricanes/Panthers "hardest playoff outs," Avalanche "best in-season but health-dependent," Golden Knights "unbeatable at home."
3. Hockey's spread is almost always 1.5 goals, with a higher upset rate than basketball.
4. The goaltender is the most important, most overlooked variable — always check before you bet.
5. Back-to-backs are the biggest source of upsets, especially away teams on a back-to-back.
6. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
