Big platforms pile their resources onto the mainstream sports, so niche-sport markets often go unattended—for ordinary players, that is both a trap and an opportunity. Each of the seven sports below has its own guide; read the positioning first, then dive in.

## Esports

The market structure closest to traditional sports (handicap, totals, map winner), but player form swings hard and lower-tier leagues carry match-fixing risk. Best for people who only bet the games they follow long-term.

[Esports guide](/niche-sports/esports-betting-guide)

## Darts

Simple markets, short matches, fast pace—great for beginners. But the top players are closely matched, upsets are common, and emotion matters a lot.

[Darts guide](/niche-sports/darts-betting-guide)

## Snooker

Relatively transparent and slow-paced—suited to people willing to do the work. Long formats have fewer upsets; short formats have more.

[Snooker guide](/niche-sports/snooker-betting-guide)

## Tennis

The niche sport closest to the mainstream, with mature markets. The key variables are surface type, injuries and live form.

[Tennis guide](/niche-sports/tennis-betting-guide)

## F1

One of the most uniquely structured markets—no spreads, you bet finishing positions and qualifying. The key variable is the "car + driver" double factor.

[F1 guide](/niche-sports/f1-betting-guide)

## UFC / MMA

The fastest-growing combat betting sport, with seven-figure handles on main events. The key variables are grappler vs striker, the weight cut/weigh-in and camp preparation.

[UFC guide](/niche-sports/mma-betting-guide)

## Cricket

The world's second-biggest betting market, with India as its home. The key variables are pitch, weather and who bats first; beginners should start with T20 and ODI.

[Cricket guide](/niche-sports/cricket-betting-guide)

Read through all seven, pick one to master and do not spread yourself thin. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
