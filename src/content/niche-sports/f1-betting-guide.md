F1 (Formula One) is one of the fastest-growing individual betting sports, and it is a peculiar one: what looks like a duel between drivers is actually a duel between teams' technology. Its market logic is completely different from other sports—no spread, no over/under—you bet on finishing positions and on "who is faster".

This page first explains what F1 is and who the strong teams are, then how to bet it.

## Start here: F1 is not a "driver duel", it is "car + driver"

F1's most fundamental difference from other sports: **the driver only decides part of it, the car's performance decides the other part.**

- A fast car plus a good driver is what contends for titles.
- However strong the driver, they cannot win in a slow car—that is F1's iron law.

So when you bet F1, you are not judging "how good is this driver", but "how fast is this driver plus this car right now". That is why the "constructor market" (which team wins) is just as important as the "driver market".

## Seasonal rhythm: 24 races, March to December

F1 runs one season a year, and the 2026 season has 24 Grands Prix from March to December, spanning the globe:

- **<details class="term"><summary>Qualifying</summary><span class="term-note">The Saturday session before the race where drivers set single-lap times to decide Sunday's starting grid. The higher you qualify, the bigger the race advantage.</span></details> (Saturday)**: decides the race's starting order, and qualifying results directly shape the race.
- **Race (Sunday)**: the main event, deciding positions and points.

**Advice for beginners: F1's qualifying is must-read information before you bet.** Because overtaking in F1 is hard, the better you qualify, the bigger the race advantage—qualifying results often already foreshadow most of the race.

## Who the strong teams are (2026 season, with stories to help you remember)

The 2026 F1 season has a story for the history books:

- **Mercedes**: the 2026 season's absolute hegemon. Their Italian driver **Andrea Kimi Antonelli** locked up the championship at 20 years old, becoming the youngest world champion in F1 history—a "genius arrives out of nowhere" script. Mercedes also took the constructors' title.
- **Ferrari**: F1's most legendary and most popular team. In 2026 **Lewis Hamilton** joined and took the 105th pole position of his career—one of the greatest F1 drivers ever (7 titles), and his Ferrari move was the year's biggest story.
- **Red Bull**: the team of **Max Verstappen**, the dominant driver of recent years, who regained form late in 2026 by winning Bahrain, Singapore and Mexico.
- **McLaren**: led by **Lando Norris**, who won Hungary and the Netherlands in 2026—a steady title-contending force.

**Memory hook**: Mercedes "new king crowned", Ferrari "legend + popularity", Red Bull "Verstappen back to form", McLaren "steady dark horse".

## The two core markets

F1's markets differ from traditional sports; the core is these two:

- **Driver market (who wins / who podiums)**: bet a driver to win a given race, or to win the championship. This is F1's most common market.
- **Constructor market (constructor champion / head-to-head)**: bet a team to win, or a head-to-head between two teams.

There are also derivatives: **qualifying position** (who takes pole), **retirement markets** (who retires), and so on. Beginners should start with "driver to win" and "constructor head-to-head".

## Key variables: qualifying and car performance

F1's two most crucial variables are also the ones beginners most often ignore:

1. **Qualifying position**: overtaking is hard in F1, so qualifying directly sets your starting spot. A driver who takes <details class="term"><summary>pole position</summary><span class="term-note">First in qualifying, starting Sunday's race from the front. Because overtaking is hard in F1, pole position massively boosts a driver's chances.</span></details> (first) greatly improves their race odds. **Always check qualifying results before betting.**
2. **Car performance / upgrades**: F1 teams upgrade their cars throughout the season. A successful upgrade makes a team faster; a failed one can ruin several races. This is the "equipment variable"—it is not the driver who changed, it is the car.

**These two are F1's biggest differences from other sports.** Other sports look at the "person"; F1 also looks at the "car".

## Common mistakes

- Only looking at a driver's fame and not the car's performance—in F1 even a great driver cannot win in a slow car.
- Betting the race without checking qualifying results—qualifying already foreshadows most of the race.
- Treating "Verstappen is a dominant driver" as if he wins every race, and ignoring his early-season slump and Red Bull's car form swings.
- Ignoring mid-season upgrade cycles and betting on impressions from months ago.

## What this page should leave you with

1. F1 is the double variable of "car + driver"—do not only look at the person, look at the car.
2. Qualifying is must-read information before you bet; it foreshadows most of the race.
3. Memory hook: Mercedes new king crowned, Ferrari legend + popularity, Red Bull Verstappen back to form, McLaren steady dark horse.
4. Start with "driver to win" and "constructor head-to-head", and avoid high-variance markets like retirements.
5. Watch mid-season car upgrades; do not bet on old impressions.
6. Only stake money you can afford to lose.
