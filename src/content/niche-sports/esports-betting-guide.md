Esports is one of the fastest-growing betting categories, with annual handle already in the $40 billion range and a mostly young player base. Its markets look like traditional sports (handicap, totals, match winner), but the traps underneath are completely different—patches, roster moves and match-fixing can each destroy your read.

This page walks you through the full esports landscape and top teams, then the traps beginners stumble into most.

## Start here: esports is not one sport, it is many

Unlike "soccer" or "basketball", esports splits into several completely different games, and each has its own patch logic, format and top teams:

- **League of Legends (LOL)**: the biggest esport by volume and market depth, with Korea's LCK and China's LPL as the top regions.
- **CS2 (Counter-Strike 2)**: the esport closest to a "traditional sports" logic—strong teams are stable and variance is comparatively low.
- **DOTA2**: home of the highest-prize single tournament (The International), but its landscape is more fragmented.

**Lesson one for beginners: only bet the games you genuinely follow long-term.** You only have real judgement when you know a game's players, teams and patch rhythm; otherwise your "analysis" is just guessing off the odds. Do not spread yourself thin—pick one and master it.

## The seasonal rhythm, using LOL as an example

LOL has a clear annual rhythm, with the World Championship (often called "Worlds") as the year's climax:

- **Spring / Summer splits**: each region's own league—the densest schedule of the year.
- **MSI (Mid-Season Invitational)**: in May, the regional champions clash—the year's first international major.
- **World Championship (Worlds)**: October to November, the year's peak. The 2026 S16 World Championship runs from October 15 to November 14 in North America, with the final at Barclays Center in Brooklyn, New York.

**Advice for beginners: do not fixate only on the Worlds final.** Worlds has the most attention and the hottest books, but also the most upsets and emotional swings. To practice, start with mid-season regular-season matches—they are steadier.

## Who the strong teams are (the LOL world picture)

LOL's world picture is clear: Korea's LCK is the absolute hegemon, and China's LPL is the challenger.

- **T1 (Korea)**: the greatest organization in LOL history, with six world titles (the most ever), and core player Faker widely regarded as the "god of esports". Their 2025 title in Chengdu showed the dynasty is still alive. Betting them wins pays little, but they are the default favorite in every major.
- **Gen.G (Korea)**: two world titles, the strongest Korean team behind T1. HLE won the 2026 MSI, but Gen.G is a long-running title contender.
- **Hanwha Life (HLE, Korea)**: 2026 MSI champions with a star-studded roster—the fastest-rising Korean team of the last two years.
- **AL (Anyone's Legend, China)**: 2026 LPL champions, the new power in the Chinese region.
- **BLG, TES, IG (China)**: LPL's established powers. IG was the first team to win a world title for the LPL (in 2018), TES has star player JackeyLove, and BLG is one of the strongest LPL reps in recent years.

**Memory hook**: T1 is the "six-title dynasty", LCK is the "top region", LPL is the "challenger", and AL is "China's new power".

## Understand the three basic market types

- **Handicap (game/map handicap)**: the favorite concedes games to the underdog; you bet whether they win by more than that handicap.
- **Totals (over/under)**: whether total kills, total games or total maps go over or under a number.
- **<details class="term"><summary>Map winner</summary><span class="term-note">An esports match is played across several maps. Betting a single map's winner is far more volatile than betting the whole match.</span></details>**: a single map's winner—more volatile than the whole match.

Beginners should start with "match winner" and "handicap", and stay away from high-variance single-map markets at first.

## The key variable: patches and roster moves matter more than results

Esports' biggest variable is the patch and the roster. One balance update can instantly weaken a strong team; one key player leaving or being benched can overturn all your previous reads. Before betting, check:

- When was the last patch, and did it change key champions/roles?
- Did the starting roster change, and is the starting player in?

## Avoiding traps: only touch the top leagues

Top leagues (like LOL's LCK/LPL, and CS2's <details class="term"><summary>Major</summary><span class="term-note">CS2's top-tier official tournament tier, with mature anti-match-fixing controls—the safest esports level to bet.</span></details>) have mature anti-match-fixing mechanisms. In lower-tier leagues and online cups, match-fixing risk is real and hard to verify from outside. The best move for beginners is to **only touch the top leagues** and dial the risk down.
