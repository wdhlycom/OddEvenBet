Darts is a great sport for beginners to practice on: simple markets, short matches, fast pace—a match is over in a dozen or so minutes. But precisely because it is fast, emotion's effect on players is amplified, and upsets are especially common.

## 1. Markets: simple and direct

- **Match winner**: who wins—the most basic.
- **Handicap**: the stronger player concedes legs to the weaker one; you bet whether they win by more than that handicap.
- **Total <details class="term"><summary>180s</summary><span class="term-note">A 180 is darts' maximum single turn: three darts all in the triple 20, worth 180 points. Betting the total 180s across a match is a side bet that leans toward luck.</span></details>**: how many "180s" (the maximum score in a turn) are thrown across the match.
- **Highest <details class="term"><summary>checkout</summary><span class="term-note">A checkout is the dart that reduces your score exactly to zero to win a leg. A higher checkout means a flashier finish. Betting the highest checkout is fun, but not a market to stake heavily.</span></details>**: who throws the highest checkout.

Beginners should start with match winner and handicap; total 180s and highest checkout are more like "fun side bets"—enjoy them but do not stake heavily.

## 2. Fast pace, good for practice

Darts matches are short, and a player may go through several rounds in an evening. For beginners the benefit is "fast feedback"—you quickly see whether your read was right, and losses are clear and honest.

But do not confuse practice with a steady income. Fast does not mean easy to predict.

## 3. The top players are closely matched, so upsets are common

Among darts' elite, the gaps between players are much smaller than in other sports. A matchup that looks like a sure thing by ranking frequently gets overturned by one hot leg from the underdog.

- Upsets are common—do not stake heavily just because the ranking is high.
- Emotion matters a lot: darts depends heavily on feel, and a player's form swings far more than the surface numbers suggest on any given day.

## 4. Common mistakes

- Staking heavily by ranking and ignoring the fact that upsets are common.
- Getting carried away by the fast pace and firing off too many bets in one evening, losing control of stakes.
- Betting total 180s and highest checkout as your main markets, when they are closer to random.

## What this page should leave you with

1. Start with match winner and handicap; treat total 180s and highest checkout as side bets.
2. Top players are closely matched and upsets are common—ranking does not mean a sure win.
3. The fast pace is good practice, but do not treat it as a steady income.
4. Only stake money you can afford to lose—darts' fast pace is what most easily makes you lose control.
