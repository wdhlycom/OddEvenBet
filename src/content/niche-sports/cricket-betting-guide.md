Cricket is a sport that most readers—especially in the Chinese-speaking world—find completely unfamiliar, but its scale is enormous. It is the second-biggest sports betting market globally (behind only soccer), with its core players in India, Pakistan, the UK, Australia and South Africa. In the first quarter of 2026, cricket trading volume on Polymarket passed $100 million.

If you work an English-language market, cricket is something you cannot ignore. This page takes you from zero to understanding how to bet it.

## Start here: cricket's scale and its home

What makes cricket special is that it "looks niche but is actually a giant". India has over a billion cricket viewers, and by "value per match" the Indian Premier League (IPL) is the world's second-richest sports league (behind only the NFL)—its media rights for one cycle sold for $6.4 billion.

**In one sentence: the English-speaking world's cricket audience is massively underrated by outsiders.** For anyone working an English-language market, that is an opportunity—a huge-traffic pocket with far less competition than soccer or basketball.

## The IPL and the strong teams (with stories to help you remember)

Cricket splits into "national teams" and "club leagues". The most useful for beginners is the IPL (Indian Premier League, every March to May):

- **Royal Challengers Bengaluru (RCB)**: champions in 2025 and 2026 back to back, only the third team in IPL history to successfully defend a title. Virat Kohli is an Indian cricket legend who led them to the 2026 title with an unbeaten 75 in the final—a story of a veteran still carrying the flag.
- **Mumbai Indians**: tied with Chennai Super Kings for the most titles in history (five each)—one of the IPL's traditional powerhouses.
- **Chennai Super Kings**: another five-title powerhouse, veteran and steady.

**Memory hook**: RCB is the "reigning back-to-back champion"; Mumbai Indians and Chennai are the "five-title traditional powerhouses".

## Separate the three formats first

Cricket is not one game—it is three formats with completely different rhythms:

- **T20 (Twenty20)**: the shortest, about three hours a match. Fastest pace, most upsets, closest to "entertainment". The IPL is T20.
- **ODI (One Day International)**: about eight hours. Balanced; the World Cup's main format.
- **Test**: the longest, up to five days. Slowest pace, strongest teams' advantage most stable, fewest upsets.

**Beginners should start with T20 and ODI, and leave Test for people with long-term cricket knowledge.** A Test match runs five days, has too many variables, and its market logic differs hugely from other sports.

## Cricket's unique key variables

Cricket outcomes depend on variables no other sport has:

1. **Pitch**: the pitch's grass, hardness and moisture vary by ground and decide whether conditions favor batters or bowlers. This is the single most important judgement in cricket betting.
2. **Weather**: cricket is extremely weather-sensitive; rain can directly shorten the match or even change the handicap.
3. **The toss** (<details class="term"><summary>toss</summary><span class="term-note">The pre-match coin toss decides who bats first. Sometimes the result matters more than the two teams' strength.

**These three are cricket's biggest differences from other sports.** If you do not understand pitch and weather, you are effectively betting blind.

## An underrated market: India

Cricket's real value is India. The IPL is the world's most valuable cricket league and one of the most active sports betting markets on earth.

- India has over a billion cricket viewers—enormous scale.
- But India's gambling law is complex, and much of it sits in a grey zone.

**If you do affiliate marketing in an English-language market, cricket (especially the IPL) can drive a huge amount of precise traffic—but pay extra attention to India's compliance issues.**

## Common mistakes

- Betting Test with a T20 mindset, getting the rhythm logic completely wrong.
- Ignoring pitch and weather, which is effectively betting blind.
- Skipping the "who bats first" variable and giving up initiative on favorable pitches.
